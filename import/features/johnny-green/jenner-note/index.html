Jenner family note on Johnny Green, 1960

A short family recollection that Johnny Green was found on the ground in 1960, re-erected by the Methodist youth club, and painted from a red coat to a green one.

Working copy of an unpublished note kept with Meredith Satchell’s papers, file Collections/Jenner/Johnny Green.rtf. It is not a contemporary 1960 newspaper report. Burra In The News confirms Rev. R. S. Jenner’s induction in January 1960 and his transfer to Kapunda in January 1964; it does not report this repair.

Transcription

Johnny Green

In 1960 Rev Richard Jenner was Methodist Minister for Burra. Richard noticed that Johnny Green who was mounted on a steel pole at the entrance to the mine was on the ground with the guy wires holding the pole broken. As no one was doing anything about it, Richard decided to make it a church youth club project to get him standing again. Up until this time Johnny Green had a red coat and Richard thought it made him look like a communist, so before he was stood up he painted a green coat on him and green coat remains on him to this day,

The comma at the end is in the file.

How this article uses it

The Johnny Green feature treats the drop, the youth-club repair, and the red-to-green coat as a Jenner-family recollection, not as a fact independently confirmed in the papers. The green coat is a later tradition, not a nineteenth-century fact.
