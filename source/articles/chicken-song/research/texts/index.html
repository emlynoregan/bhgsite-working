Diplomatic pile for When I was a chicken. Lineation follows the source named. Do not silently regularise Ward’s dialect to the generic family.

Elizabeth A. Ward, Burra, 1936

From “That there Man,” Burra Record, 13 October 1936. Working transcription; check the scan before print quotation. Full piece: That there Man.

When I was a chicken no bigger ner a hen
Cappen Jinimy hit me
An’ I hit ’im agen.
Harry Cock came down to order me out
I up with me han’
An’ I give ’im a clout
Cappen Dick came down to give me a whack.
I had a stick
An’ I broke his back.

Alexander Smith, 1865

A Summer in Skye, vol. 2. A forgeman sings; this is already an adult comic performance in print.

When I was a chicken as big as a hen,
My mother ’ot me, an’ I ’ot her agen;
My father came in for to see the r-r-rrow,
So I lifted my fist, an’ I ’ot him a clow.

Archive.org.

G. F. Northall, 1892

English Folk-Rhymes (London: Kegan Paul, Trench, Trübner, 1892), p. 318. Northall calls the rhyme common in the Midlands. The South Cheshire father-line quoted in the Ward article is “My father came in, and he order’d me out.” Pull the surrounding stanza from the scan before treating any extra lines as Northall’s.

Tricia’s family version (Folktrax)

Grandmother born Australia 1889. Printed by Folktrax under “Over the Garden Wall.” See Folktrax notes.

When I was a chicken as big as a hen
Me mother hit me so I hit her again
Me father came out and gave me a clout
So I upped with the hammer and flattened him out
Me cruel mother killed me
Me poor father ate me
Me little sister sucked me bones and laid them on a log

Bill Smith, Bridgnorth, 1980

Chicken stanza inside “Over the Garden Wall.” Full garden-wall frame and the later school-yard verse: Folktrax / Musical Traditions notes.
