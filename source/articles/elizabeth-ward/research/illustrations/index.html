Recommended feature sequence

  1. Opening image: Burra Salvation Army Corps, 1905, captioned “Annie Ward, probably Elizabeth Ann Ward” and accompanied by the identification caveat.
  2. Childhood: Kooringa panorama, 1872.
  3. Mine memory: Morphett's engine house/Johnny Green photograph, followed by the Gill view as historical context.
  4. Public voice: a newspaper facsimile of “Cleaning Footpaths” or the 1885 Salvation Army letter.
  5. Rhyme: an enlarged clipping detail from “That there Man.”

Images embedded in the research draft

The current article embeds the 1872 Kooringa panorama, the 1905 Salvation Army corps photograph, Morphett's pumphouse and “Johnny Green,” and the smelting-works ruins. They are loaded from their existing archival delivery URLs so the research draft can be read visually. Before public publication, replace these with cleared, locally served image files at an agreed resolution and apply the collection holder's preferred credit lines.

Identity and rights cautions

  • “Annie Ward” is probably Elizabeth Ann Ward, but the photograph is not a confirmed portrait. The surviving digital evidence shows that Elizabeth was a prominent unmarried member of this precise corps immediately before the photograph; it does not show that she used “Annie” or exclude every other possible Ward. Retain the qualification unless newly digitised records or an independently identified portrait resolves the question.
  • Hosting is not proof of copyright ownership or permission to reproduce. Obtain the collection holder's current terms for each image.
  • Do not use a later publication scan when the holding institution can supply the original image.
  • Newspaper facsimiles should identify the newspaper and date, not the hosting platform.
