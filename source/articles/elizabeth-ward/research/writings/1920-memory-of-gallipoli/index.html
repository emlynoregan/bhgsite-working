Original Poetry. A Memory of Gallipoli.

[An incident recorded by "Trooper
Bluegum" at the Dardanelles.]

'Tis Sabbath eve ; yet Sabbath calm
Is far from that dread spot ;
Great guns roar out incessant boom
Whilst shrapnel shell and shot

From rifle fire fall in a constant rain,
Tearing great pitfalls in the trembling
ground.
A smoke-pall covers hill and plain,
Which echo with war's awful sound.
But hark ! a different note is heard
Above the roar and din ;
Was that the sound of sacred word ?
Nay, it could not have been,
Yet surely someone speaks ; that voice
Has a familiar sound.
Who could hold service there from
choice,
Near that wild shell-torn ground ?
Upon a sloping hillside stand
About two thousand men,
And Padre Mac holds in his hand
His bible. Once again
He speaks : "Oh men ! prepare
For what the morn may bring."
Then kneels in short but earnest prayer,
Then silence — "Padre sing."
" What will ye hae lads ? sing awa' "
The sweet familiar strain
Sings out in volume loud and clear,
In solemn grand refrain.
Never have they felt before
All those words for them contain
Memories of days of yore,
Visions bright they see again.
Still they sing whilst crashing shells
Tear o'er head and bullets ping.
"Cover my defenceless head
With the shadow of thy wing.
Hide me, oh my Saviour, hide,
Till the storm of life is past ;
Safe into the haven guide,
Oh receive my soul at last."
Night comes on, but still they sing ;
Are the angels there on guard ?
Round that band with outspread wing,
Keep they holy watch and ward ?
"Rock of Ages, cleft for me,
Let me hide myself in Thee.
In my hands no price I bring,
Simply to Thy cross I cling."
Sure such worship ne'er before
Witnessed on earth hath been,
Silently they steal away,
Tears in many eyes are seen.
Some to ponder, some to pray,
Voicing softly o'er again :
"Rock of Ages, cleft for me,
Let me hide myself in Thee."
14-4-20. E. A. WARD,
Burra, South Australia.
