GALLIPOLI.

Land of the brave ! Thy scarred hills
In silent witness stand
Of deeds that deep each heart still thrills,
Deeds, of the noble band.

In days long past heroes arose,
And history's pages glow
With cherished names, who conquered
foes,
But we have heroes now !
They are our sons who fought on thee,
Land of the brave ! Gallipoli !
Land of the brave ! For brave were those
Who landing in that morning grey,
Scaled thy grim heights to rout the foe
Whilst bursting shells obstruct their
way,
And brave were those who on the sea
Launched boat on boat to bear to land
Those khaki'd hosts, strong men and
true,
Of glowing heart and firm right hand ;
Can'st thou forget, oh murmuring sea,
The landing on Gallipoli?
Land of the brave ! Who heard the call
Of mother-land across the wave,
And answered gladly, leaving all
They prized on earth to help and save ;
from peaceful home, from house and
store,
From farm and mart, from wife and
child ;
They learn the grim, stern art of War
On thy bleak headlands, awful, wild,
They were oursons who fought ou thee —
Peninsula stern — Gallipoli !
Land of the brave ! On thee was won '
A Nation's great undying fame,
All climes and creeds beneath the sun
Thrilled at the sound of Australia's
name !
On thee, a standard high was set,
From which her sons have never quailed
In field where'er the' foe was met
Courage and Honour there prevailed,
A Nation's fame was made on thee
The Anzac's land— Gallipoli !
Land of the brave ! One common cause
Filled every heart with martial fire.
Colour and creed forgot their laws,
To brotherhood doth each aspire ;
From East and West : The Ghurka
brave.
And Maori fierce their forces join,
Blighty and Aussie henceforth have
One hope, one combat, one design
Aud Britain's flag floats proud and free
O'er one and all — Gallipoli !
Land of the brave ! Ye peoples who
By word or deed disloyally strive
The seed of discord basely sow.
Know that such evil shall not thrive,
Ye cannot from the Mother turn
Her children's hearts where'er they
be.
They rally at her bugle-call
From distant lands across the sea ;
Deep in each heart this lesson burn.
Thus hast thou taught — Gallipoli.
Land of the brave ! Mid scorching heat
Hunger and thirst were nobly borne,
Marches with weary, bleeding feet.
Clothes rent with cruel tearing thorn,
And winter winds whose icy breath
Freeze life-blood in their chilling veins
Trenches their home, and o'er them
Death
Dropping from every hill and plain.
Oh brave strong hearts : All Hail to
Thee
Who fought that fight— Gallipoli !
Land of the brave ! Where'er on Earth
Or air, or sea. or native land,
Where'er a soul of English birth
Praise of great deeds of noble band
Shall there be sung : Mothers shall tell
To infants nursed upon their knee
O how their fathers fought and fell
In glorious cause to make man free,
Thy name shall in remembrance be
Whilst Nations live — Gallipoli !
Land of the brave ! And not the least
Who day and night so ceaseless ply
In aid of man. The noble beast
The horse and mule so patiently
Up the steep path with watei climb.
And bear to succour wounded men
So stout of heart, so strong of limb
They haul the gun. Due homage
then
To them we pay who wrought on thee
A service great — Gallipoli !
Land of the brave ! O'er hill and vale
Gleams the Red Cross of Sacrifice,
Thy whispering winds breathe oft the
tale
That as to Heaven an incense rise ;

Land of the brave ! Ah who shall speak
Of that strange last assembly there—
Tears trickle down the bronzed cheek
Of men who did not quail in War,
Why should each bosom heave and swell ?
Why should such strange motion be?
The time has come to say 'Farewell'
To comrades in their last long sleep
And thee,, A last "Farewell."
Home of our dead— Gallipoli !
Land of our dead ! In peaceful sleep
They lie in heroes graves
Flowers o'er the mounded earth now
creep,
Long grasses gently wave,
The low winds sing the parting hymn,
The stars look softly down
The sea moans forth their requiem,
Whilst over all is thrown
The moon's bright gleam, and there
with thee
Our dead we leave — Gallipoli !
Land of the brave ! No monument
Is needed for onr gallant slain,
Their deeds the grandest monument
That e'er was raised to valiant men.
The small white cross that marks the lot
Where'er each hero lowly lies,
To all becomes a sacred spot
And honored in each Briton's eyes.
Their monument is found in thee,
Land of tbe brave ; Gallipoli.
Land of the brave ! Time steals away
And gently softens grief and path.
Yet many hearts are sore to-day
For those who will not come again.
Oh, God in heaven, we ask of Thee
To heal each heart by grief oppressed,
To hallow each fond memory
And soothe each tortured, aching
breast.
Deep in our hearts will ever be.
Our men who fell. — Galipoli !
Land of tbe brave ! By blood outpoured ;
By life for us laid down.
Suffering and pain, brave souls endured,
By many a martyr's crown ;
By bravest deeds that man may do
On land in air and sea ;
Whilst years roll on, the memory true
Will ever turn to thee
Whilst life shall last and sun endure.
IMMORTALISED — Gallipoli !
Elizabeth A. Ward, Kooringa-, Burra, S. A.
