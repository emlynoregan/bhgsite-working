3BIGINAL POETRY.

HOW THE HIGHLANDERS BURIED
THEIR DEAD.

Slowly the sun is setting.
Twilight is deepening fast.
Hushed is the cannon's roaring
Stilled the shrill bugle's blast ;

See, forms moving silently
With slow, heavy tread,
Ah ! 'tis the Highland Brigade
Burying their dead.
Soul moving strains are stealing
On the still evening air.
Like most flowers they have wither'd
Comrades and friends are mere ;
' Man cometh up like a flower,'
Solemnly ibe words are said,
Aye ! 'tis ihe Highland Brigade
Burying their dead.
Grandly lay each warrior
Tartan enshrouded there ;
Ceased all their earthly warfare —
List 1 'tis the voice of prayer ;
Then the deep slogan's pealing
While last farewells are said,
Tears down bronzed faces stealing
Burying their dead.
Trembling hands are spunkling
The light sod o'er their breast,
Comrades all so tenderly,
Lay Them to their long rest ;
Playing their last dirge o'er them
While sadly droops each head,
Mournfully the Highlanders
Have buried their dead.
? ? ?
Messages to the homeland
Tell of the loved one gone,
With bowed head the aged mother
Prayeth ' Thy will be done,'
Vainly shall child and wife
Listen for husband's tread —
Far away have the Highlanders
Buried their dead.
Sleeping there so calmly
In warm, soft mother earth,
Proudly thou wilt think of them,
Ob, thou land of their birth ;
Caring tenderly ior the loved ones
Of thee shall it be well said,
Britain hath, aye remembered
Her buried dead.
E. A. Ward.
Burra, February 24, 1900.
