Finding

Elizabeth Ann Ward is the strongest—and probably the correct—identification of the woman captioned “Annie Ward” in the 1905 photograph of the Burra Salvation Army Corps. The identification is not proved. No located digital source calls Elizabeth “Annie,” and the photograph's caption supplies neither initials nor a family relationship.

The defensible publication wording is therefore “Annie Ward, probably Elizabeth Ann Ward”, followed by a brief explanation of the uncertainty. It should not be presented as an uncontested portrait of Elizabeth.

The photograph

The Ian Auhl Collection photograph is captioned “BURRA SALVATION ARMY CORPS 1905.” Its identification reads:

> Back: Jack Hemmings, ____ Wilton, Mrs Oswald, Annie Ward, ____, Fred Kemble.
> Front: ____, ____, ____, Evelyn Pearce, Millie Pearce, Sara Geake.

“Annie Ward” is therefore fourth from the left in the back row. The hosted catalogue record says the image was donated by F. Atkins, but it does not state when, by whom, or from what evidence the names were supplied.

Search for another Annie Ward

An exact-phrase search for “Annie Ward” across the digitised run of the Burra Record returned only two results:

  1. an 1895 Booborowie item, naming an Annie Ward in a local list there; and
  2. a 1903 advertisement in which the apparent match is OCR splitting the surname “Hayward” in “Olive Annie Hayward.”

Neither result identifies a second Annie Ward in Burra or Kooringa. Searches for “Miss Ward” and “Sister Ward,” by contrast, repeatedly retrieve the documented Elizabeth and her Salvation Army activity.

This negative result strengthens the identification only modestly. Trove OCR can miss names, not every relevant record was published in a newspaper, and a visitor or a woman recorded under another formal name could escape the search.

One tempting result must be excluded. An 1868 Adelaide Observer school-prize report appears in snippets to associate “Annie Ward” with Clare, where Elizabeth was born. Reading the full article shows that the named pupil belonged to the Willaston School section, not the Clare section. It is not evidence about Elizabeth.

Evidence still missing

No located digital source:

  • calls Elizabeth Ann Ward “Annie”;
  • gives the photographed woman initials, parents, address or occupation;
  • explains the provenance and date of the typed identification; or
  • supplies a second, independently identified portrait of Elizabeth.

The name “Annie” is a familiar form of Ann, so it is compatible with Elizabeth's middle name, but linguistic compatibility is not biographical proof. The caption might also preserve another woman's formal name, a nickname, or an error.

Assessment

The identification rests on convergence, not a single equation of names. Elizabeth was not merely one Ward somewhere in Burra: she was an unmarried, conspicuous and long-serving member of the photographed corps, documented in that role from 1885 and explicitly profiled in 1904. No competing Burra or Kooringa “Annie Ward” has emerged from the digitised local newspaper.

That combination justifies moving beyond “possibly Elizabeth Ann Ward” to “probably Elizabeth Ann Ward.” It does not justify removing the qualifier. Further work can now be limited to noticing newly digitised sources—especially an independently captioned portrait, a corps list, or a record explicitly pairing “Elizabeth” and “Annie”—rather than making the identity a prerequisite for the article.
